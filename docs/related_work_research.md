# Audio LLMs and the high-stakes fragility of machine emotion perception

**Audio Large Language Models that interpret human emotion from voice are proliferating into life-altering decisions — from depression screening to fraud detection to hiring — yet published research demonstrates these systems' emotion perception can be flipped by imperceptible adversarial perturbations, with accuracy drops exceeding 69%.** This convergence of rapid deployment and demonstrated vulnerability creates an urgent security landscape: billions of voice interactions are now processed by emotion-aware AI, and the scientific foundation for adversarial robustness has barely begun to form. The proliferation spans at least 20 open-source Audio LLMs, dozens of commercial products, and deployment across seven critical sectors — healthcare, finance, law enforcement, education, hiring, automotive, and insurance — affecting hundreds of millions of users.

---

## The open-source Audio LLM ecosystem now treats emotion as a first-class capability

Between 2024 and early 2026, at least **20 major open-source Audio LLMs** launched with varying degrees of emotion perception capability. The most important trend is that emotion recognition has shifted from a niche add-on to an explicitly claimed, benchmarked, and marketed feature.

**Kimi-Audio** (Moonshot AI, April 2025) achieves state-of-the-art  **59.13% accuracy on MELD emotion recognition** , processing audio through a hybrid architecture that combines continuous Whisper acoustic vectors with discrete semantic tokens fed into a Qwen2.5-7B backbone. **WavLLM** (Microsoft Research, 2024) uses a dual-encoder design — Whisper for content plus WavLM for paralinguistic features — explicitly designed to preserve emotion and prosody, scoring  **59.8% on IEMOCAP emotion recognition** . **Qwen2-Audio** (Alibaba, July 2024) lists speech emotion recognition as a core training task and scores competitively across AudioBench's emotion evaluation suites. **SALMONN** (Tsinghua/ByteDance, 2023) combines Whisper and BEATs audio encoders to capture "both linguistic and paralinguistic information," including emotion recognition as an explicit capability.

**GLM-4-Voice** (Zhipu AI, October 2024) takes a distinct approach, processing speech natively through a 175-bps speech tokenizer rather than routing through ASR, explicitly targeting "emotion perception, emotional resonance, and emotional expression." **Moshi** (Kyutai, September 2024), the first real-time full-duplex spoken dialogue model at 200ms latency, claims to "understand emotions and other non-verbal aspects of communication," though formal SER benchmarking is absent. Later entrants like **Qwen2.5-Omni** (March 2025), **Step-Audio** (2025), and **MiniCPM-o 4.5** (2025) further expand the space.

The benchmark ecosystem has matured in parallel. **AudioBench** (NAACL 2025) evaluates emotion recognition on IEMOCAP and MELD across 26+ datasets, finding that "AudioLLMs generally outperform Whisper+LLM pipelines but need further improvement" on paralinguistic tasks. **Dynamic-SUPERB Phase 2** expanded to 180 tasks including emotion recognition. **SALMon** specifically evaluates paralinguistic perception including emotion, and **SOVA-Bench** (Interspeech 2025) tests emotion recognition alongside speech conversation quality. A critical finding from recent research is that many models take "lexical shortcuts" — inferring emotion from text content rather than acoustic-prosodic cues — raising questions about what these systems actually perceive versus what they infer.

## Commercial products embed emotion AI into systems serving billions

The commercial deployment of voice emotion perception is far more extensive than the open-source research ecosystem suggests. **GPT-4o** (OpenAI, May 2024), now serving  **900 million weekly active users** , processes audio natively and "senses and responds to humor, sarcasm, and more" through vocal variation analysis. **Google Gemini 2.5 Flash Native Audio** explicitly supports emotion detection with documented API endpoints for detecting speaker emotion states, enabling "affective dialogue" that can "automatically de-escalate stressful calls." These are not research prototypes — they are production systems processing billions of daily interactions.

**Hume AI** represents the most explicitly emotion-focused commercial platform, having raised **$50 million in Series B funding** (March 2024) to build its Empathic Voice Interface (EVI). EVI analyzes tune, rhythm, and timbre to detect dozens of emotional states — from admiration to contempt to sarcasm — and adjusts responses accordingly. Backed by 8+ publications in Nature and Nature Human Behavior, Hume AI serves customers including Snap, SoftBank, and Mount Sinai hospital. In China, **ByteDance's Doubao** (159 million monthly users, processing 16.4 trillion tokens daily) claims voice with "precise emotional expression" that is "nearly indistinguishable from human," while **iFlytek** ($3.53B revenue) integrates emotion recognition into everything from smart cockpits to educational tablets to hearing aids.

The **contact center industry** represents the largest commercial deployment surface. **Uniphore** ($870M total funding, $2.5B valuation) acquired Emotion Research Lab and provides real-time voice emotion analysis to JPMorgan Chase, Vodafone, and DHL. **Cogito** (now Verint) provides dynamic emotional intelligence scoring on a 1–10 scale for live calls, with clients reporting  **16% increases in first-call resolution** . **CallMiner's Eureka** analyzes tone, tempo, and acoustics across 100% of customer interactions in 19 languages. **NICE CXone** reports that emotion-aware routing reduces escalations by  **42%** . These systems collectively process millions of calls daily, with emotion scores directly influencing agent coaching, call routing, and customer outcome decisions.

The mental health voice biomarker sector, though smaller, carries the highest stakes. **Kintsugi** developed 80%-accurate depression detection from 20 seconds of speech and found that voice screening detected moderate-to-high depression in **33% of patients** versus only **3% detected by standard questionnaires** — before shutting down due to unsustainable FDA regulatory costs ($16M over 4 years). **Sonde Health** ($35.25M funded) generates objective health scores from 30-second speech segments, partnering with Qualcomm for chipset-level embedding. **Ellipsis Health** ($76M total funding) recently launched "Sage," an AI care manager whose "Empathy Engine" adjusts tone based on detected patient emotional state.

## Seven critical sectors where emotion perception drives consequential decisions

**Financial services** deploys voice emotion analysis at the intersection of fraud detection and customer authentication. **Pindrop** analyzes 1,300+ acoustic features per call for major banks and detected a **1,337% surge in deepfake fraud attacks** in 2024 — from one every two days to seven daily. **Clearspeed** (raised $60M), originally built for U.S. Department of Defense counterintelligence, now screens insurance and banking calls for neurophysiological voice reactions, serving clients including **Zurich Insurance and Allianz** in 37 countries. Banks lose an average of  **$600,000 per voice deepfake incident** , and 91% of U.S. banks are now re-evaluating voice verification. The adversarial threat is concrete: synthetic voices can mimic calm, confident vocal characteristics to evade stress detection, while genuinely distressed but honest customers trigger false positives.

**Healthcare and mental health** represent the highest-consequence deployment. Voice biomarkers for depression screening are clinically validated (peer-reviewed study of 14,898 adults in Annals of Family Medicine, January 2025), yet no voice-based mental health diagnostic has received FDA clearance. The stakes of adversarial manipulation here are stark: in suicide risk assessment, a false negative from manipulated calm-sounding audio could be fatal, while false positives could trigger involuntary psychiatric commitment. Bias compounds this risk — Amazon Halo's tone analysis exhibited documented racial and gender bias before the product was discontinued in 2023.

**Law enforcement** continues deploying **Computer Voice Stress Analysis (CVSA)** across approximately  **3,000 U.S. law enforcement agencies** , despite the National Institute of Justice finding it performs at approximately  **50% accuracy — equivalent to a coin flip** . Documented harms include the Stephanie Crowe case (three false confessions) and the Donovan Allen case (false confession, acquitted 15 years later on DNA evidence). California's CDCR routinely used CVSA to dismiss prisoner grievance complaints until investigative journalism exposed the practice.

**HR and recruitment** saw HireVue at peak analyzing facial expressions, voice tone, and emotions across clients including Nike, Walmart, and Unilever. HireVue's retreat is instructive: they dropped facial analysis in January 2021 after their own Chief Data Scientist admitted nonverbal data contributed only  **~0.25% to predictive power** . The EPIC FTC complaint and ACLU lawsuit alleged the technology discriminated against deaf and non-white applicants. Career consultants in the U.S. and South Korea now train graduates to "perform" for AI interviewers — a direct analog to adversarial manipulation.

**Automotive** emotion detection is scaling rapidly through mandated driver monitoring systems. **Smart Eye** (which acquired Affectiva, the MIT Media Lab emotion AI pioneer) is installed in **3+ million vehicles** across 24 manufacturers. **Cerence** processes audio in  **325+ million vehicles** , though a Vice investigation revealed internal strategy to "record every movement, every glance, every smile, frown, and wrinkled brow" for monetization. Kia's "Real-time Emotion Adaptive Driving" system adjusts cabin temperature, lighting, and audio based on detected driver emotion. The EU General Safety Regulation now mandates driver monitoring in new vehicles, creating a regulatory-driven expansion of emotion sensing at scale.

## Adversarial attacks can flip emotion labels while remaining imperceptible to humans

The security research literature demonstrates that speech emotion recognition systems are fundamentally vulnerable to adversarial manipulation. **Facchinetti et al. (2024, Intelligent Computing)** systematically evaluated seven attack methods across three languages and found that all attacks significantly degraded SER performance: the **Boundary Attack** (a black-box method requiring no model access) achieved particularly alarming results. With perturbation factor ε=0.015, error rates jumped from **16% to 48%** on WaveRNN and **15% to 42.5%** on WaveCNN. A black-box speech distortion attack (EURASIP Journal, 2022) decreased CNN-MAA emotion classification accuracy by **69.38%** while the word error rate dropped only **6.24%** — meaning perturbations were near-imperceptible to listeners.

**STAA-Net** (Chang et al., IEEE 2024) introduced sparse and transferable adversarial examples that deceive SER models end-to-end, explicitly flagging healthcare and privacy-sensitive domains as vulnerable. Recent work directly targeting Audio LLMs is emerging: **"Investigating Safety Vulnerabilities of Large Audio-Language Models Under Speaker Emotional Variations"** (October 2025) found that speaker emotion inconsistently affects model safety, revealing exploitable vulnerabilities. **"Speech-Audio Compositional Attacks on Multimodal LLMs"** (November 2025) introduced SACRED-Bench showing that even state-of-the-art models like Gemini 2.5 Pro are vulnerable to complex audio-based attacks. **"Can Emotion Fool Anti-spoofing?"** (May 2025) demonstrated that emotional synthetic speech can defeat anti-spoofing systems, leading to the GEM defense framework.

The broader audio adversarial landscape provides additional attack infrastructure. **Carlini and Wagner (2018)** achieved **100% success rate** on targeted audio adversarial examples against DeepSpeech. **FAKEBOB** demonstrated **99% attack success** against speaker recognition systems. **Qin et al. (ICML 2019)** showed adversarial audio surviving over-the-air playback with **>60% success** using psychoacoustic masking. GAN-based adversarial attacks reduced deepfake detector accuracy from **98.5% to 0.08%** in graybox scenarios. Real-world exploitation is already documented: voice deepfake fraud caused a **€220,000 CEO impersonation scam** (2019) and a **$35 million UAE bank heist** (2020).

## The regulatory response recognizes the danger but lags deployment

The **EU AI Act** (Regulation 2024/1689) took the strongest stance globally, **banning emotion recognition in workplaces and education** effective February 2, 2025, with exceptions for medical or safety purposes. Penalties reach  **€35 million or 7% of global annual turnover** . European Commission guidelines explicitly prohibit call centers from using webcams and voice recognition to track employee emotions, and education institutions from inferring student attention through emotion recognition. Genesys, a major contact center platform, has already pivoted to transcript-based sentiment analysis to comply, explicitly avoiding biometric-based emotion detection.

In the United States, regulation remains fragmented. **Illinois BIPA** (the strongest U.S. biometric privacy law) covers voiceprints and has generated billions in settlements. A federal court found that McDonald's drive-through voice AI could create "voiceprints" under BIPA. **Illinois HB 3773** (effective January 2026) prohibits discriminatory AI in employment. The FTC's 2023 enforcement against Rite Aid established "algorithmic disgorgement" as a remedy — requiring deletion of models trained on biased data.

The scientific community has raised fundamental objections to the premise of emotion AI. Psychologist **Lisa Feldman Barrett** demonstrated that people scowl only  **~35% of the time when angry** , meaning emotion expression is far more variable than AI training data assumes. The **AI Now Institute** called for a ban on affect recognition in consequential decisions, citing an Association for Psychological Science review of 1,000+ papers concluding it is "very hard to reliably detect emotions from facial expressions." Large-scale bias audits consistently find disparities: ASR systems show **0.35 WER for Black speakers versus 0.19 for white speakers** (Koenecke et al., PNAS 2020), and an evaluation of 96 SER models found significant gender-wise performance gaps across individual emotions.

## Conclusion: a convergence of scale, stakes, and vulnerability

The landscape mapped here supports two core arguments with concrete evidence. First, **Audio LLMs with emotion perception are proliferating into genuinely high-stakes decisions at unprecedented scale.** The combination of 900 million ChatGPT users, 325 million vehicles with emotion-sensing AI, millions of daily contact center calls with real-time emotion scoring, and clinical voice biomarker systems screening for suicide risk creates an attack surface where adversarial manipulation of emotion perception carries severe, tangible consequences.

Second, **the threat model for adversarial attacks on emotion perception is concretely realistic.** Published attacks demonstrate that imperceptible perturbations can flip emotion labels with 50–69% accuracy degradation, black-box attacks require no model access, and over-the-air attacks survive real-world conditions. Specific exploitation scenarios are not hypothetical but are extensions of documented incidents:

* A fraudster adding imperceptible perturbations to appear calm during a Clearspeed or Pindrop voice screening, bypassing stress-based fraud flags on insurance claims or banking transactions
* An adversarial audio layer masking depression biomarkers during a Kintsugi or Sonde Health voice screening, causing a suicidal patient to be cleared as low-risk
* A candidate using adversarial audio processing during an AI-scored video interview to project optimal emotional characteristics, gaming recruitment outcomes
* An impaired driver defeating emotion-based drowsiness detection in a Smart Eye or Affectiva-equipped vehicle by manipulating audio input to the in-cabin monitoring system
* A suspect adding adversarial perturbations to defeat CVSA voice stress analysis during a law enforcement interview — though given CVSA's baseline 50% accuracy, this scenario illustrates the deeper problem that many deployed systems lack scientific validity even before adversarial attacks are considered

The gap between deployment velocity and security research is widening. Emotion AI is a multi-billion-dollar industry growing at 20%+ annually, yet the first systematic evaluation of adversarial attacks on SER was published only in 2024, no adversarial robustness benchmarks exist for Audio LLMs' emotion perception, and the EU has already moved to ban the technology in workplaces and schools. This gap — between the confidence with which emotion AI is deployed and the fragility that adversarial research reveals — defines the urgent security challenge that research in this space must address.
